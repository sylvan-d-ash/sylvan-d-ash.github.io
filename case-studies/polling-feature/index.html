Overview

Engineering tied to a clear business goal.

The feature sat inside a large consumer application used by millions of people. Its business result depended on more than client code: product decisions, application performance, advertising systems and the user experience all contributed.

My responsibility was the iOS experience. I carried it from design through implementation and release while keeping the product objective visible throughout the work.

My role

From product intent to production.

I owned the mobile implementation and release, working within the constraints of the surrounding product and commercial systems. The revenue result belonged to the complete feature; my contribution was getting its iOS experience into production.

  • Translated the polling concept into an iOS experience suitable for a high-traffic consumer application.
  • Balanced interface quality, application performance and advertising requirements.
  • Owned the work through implementation and production release rather than stopping at a prototype.

The feature generated $7M in its first year—seven times its original target. It remains my clearest example of a defined mobile-engineering contribution connected to a measurable business result.

Takeaway

The code mattered. So did shipping the right experience.

The project reinforced that implementation creates the most value when it stays connected to the product goal. Strong client code was necessary; so were the decisions and release ownership required to make it part of a complete, working product.
